\section{Ordered Pairs}

Pairing is needed to encode relations while allowing the components under
discussion to be proper classes. The two tags keep the components
distinguishable; the full construction explains how its coding is defined.

The recursive pairing construction for arbitrary classes is the Dunaev tagged
pair defined simultaneously with the Cartesian product in
\texttt{070\_ordered\_pair.tex}:
\[
(a,b)\ceq(\{0\}\times a)\cup(\{1\}\times b),
\qquad
X\times Y\ceq\cc{(x,y)}{x\in X\land y\in Y}.
\]

This is not the outer Kuratowski construction
$\{\{a\},\{a,b\}\}$. Kuratowski pairing requires its arguments to be sets and
therefore must not be substituted for tagged pairing when proper classes are
allowed. The present file is only a summary; the definitions and proofs in
\texttt{070\_ordered\_pair.tex} are authoritative.

Under well-founded set recursion and replacement, the construction is
obtained from two tags
\[
R(x)=\{\emptyclass\}\cup\cc{R(u)}{u\in x},\qquad
L(x)=\cc{R(u)}{u\in x}.
\]
Every $R(x)$ contains the empty marker, while every $L(x)$ omits it.
Membership induction proves that both tags are injective, and hence the
pair $\cc{L(a)}{a\in A}\cup\cc{R(b)}{b\in B}$ has projections recovering
$A$ and $B$. The full chapter supplies the existence, uniqueness, projection,
and sethood proofs, with these assumptions stated explicitly.

The newer Obelisk catalog uses the name \texttt{DefDunaevOrderedPair}
for a marker-tagged encoding. It has a different value on two empty
components. The longer chapter compares the two representations explicitly;
its historical recursive equations are not replaced merely because another
construction is used by a newer implementation.
