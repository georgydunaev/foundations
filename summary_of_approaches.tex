\chapter*{Guide to the Five Foundational Approaches}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Guide to the Five Foundational Approaches}

The book explores several ways to express classes, choice, partiality, and
types. This guide locates those approaches and distinguishes their local
assumptions. The term \emph{active} describes the proof-compiler development;
it is not a ranking of the mathematical value of the other sections.
Inclusion in one book does not assert a common semantics or a proved
translation between approaches.

\paragraph{How to read a section.}
First identify what its variables and terms denote, which quantifiers it
uses, and how it treats an expression with no unique value. Then read its
claims under those conventions. A total default value, an undefined term,
and an uncertain truth value are different responses to different questions.
When a section introduces a new convention, that convention governs its
argument; definitions from another approach apply only when explicitly
carried over. Labels such as \texttt{[OUTDATED]} record the history of a draft,
not a reason to dismiss the idea it investigates.

\paragraph{Notation whose scope matters.}
\begin{itemize}
\item $X\set$ is the assertion that $X$ is a set. Every class is a
subclass of $\V$, but only sets are members of $\V$.
\item $!x,\varphi$ means that at most one $x$ satisfies $\varphi$;
$\exists!x,\varphi$ additionally asserts existence. A semisingleton
may therefore be empty.
\item In the class-axiom chapter, $\Dom(F)$ records represented
arguments, including those with proper-class values; $\dom(F)$ selects
arguments whose guarded evaluations are sets. In ordinary graph
contexts, the usual domain is the set or class of first coordinates.
Check the local definition when moving between these settings.
\item A relational image collects second coordinates of records.
An image formed from evaluated values is a different operation for
families encoding proper-class values. The image chapter states which
one it uses.
\item Pair notation and descriptor notation are interfaces, not a
guarantee that two sections use identical codes or off-domain values.
The corresponding local definitions determine their interpretation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Active Mainline: Approaches 1 and 2}

The active development uses classical two-valued class theory, with
Morse--Kelley (MK) and NBG fragments distinguished by their comprehension
principles. In MK, comprehension permits quantification over classes; the
NBG restriction permits only set quantifiers within the defining formula,
while allowing class parameters. One total evaluation convention, recorded
in the local Standard~ML prototype, is unconditional union evaluation:
\[
F(x)\eqdef\union\cc{s}{(x,s)\in F}.
\]
The book explores the recursive Dunaev tagged construction for pairs of
arbitrary classes, with pairing and product defined simultaneously:
\[
(a,b)\ceq(\{0\}\times a)\cup(\{1\}\times b),
\]
This is not an outer Kuratowski pair of proper classes. The newer Obelisk
entry \texttt{DefDunaev\allowbreak OrderedPair} uses a different marker-tagged encoding.
The ordered-pair section distinguishes these constructions and their empty
pairs; a projection proof for one does not certify the recursive definition
of the other.

\paragraph{Two total evaluation conventions.}
The guarded convention in the class-axiom section and in the newer Obelisk
export \texttt{DefValueAtArg} uses
\[
F_V(x)=
\begin{cases}
\union\cc{s}{(x,s)\in F},&\text{if }\exists s,\ (x,s)\in F,\\
V,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The subscript here distinguishes conventions for the reader. Unconditional
union evaluation returns $\emptyclass$ on an absent fibre; guarded evaluation
returns $V$. Both are total and agree when a fibre is represented. Neither
is the partial description used in PFOL. The newer export also displays an
unguarded family-evaluation theorem that conflicts with its guarded
definition on the empty family. Until the source is reconciled, that theorem
must be read with an explicit domain hypothesis, not as evidence that the
two defaults coincide.

The principal sources are
\texttt{presentation.ML}, \texttt{060\_\allowbreak axioms.tex}, and
\texttt{070\_\allowbreak ordered\_\allowbreak pair.tex}.

The accompanying type discipline is
\emph{predicates/formulas-as-types, classes-as-expressions}. Class expressions
are syntactic objects; their representations in SML are not object-theoretic
classes. The chapter
\texttt{130\_\allowbreak classes\_\allowbreak as\_\allowbreak types.tex}
preserves older proposals under local \texttt{[OUTDATED]} labels alongside
its explicit set models. Its scoped predicate/type interface treats
$\operatorname{Emb}(A)$ as a supplied schematic predicate, not as an
object containing all values. The collection operation $\kappa$ retains
only set instances: collecting and then testing membership recovers a
predicate exactly when all its instances are sets. Option storage has
explicit sethood guards, and a countable-syntax diagonal argument limits
what finite closed expressions can denote. These results do not supply
a uniform internal truth predicate or a soundness theorem for every
proposed constructor.
The local discussion explains why identifying every type with $\top$
conflicts with the intended uniqueness of the term--type relation. This
objection concerns that interpretation, rather than every possible use of
classes or predicates as types.

\section{Preserved Alternative 3: Uncertain Descriptors}

\texttt{020\_\allowbreak descriptors.tex} and \texttt{180\_\allowbreak many-valued.tex} preserve a
three-valued/uncertain calculus. Its three conventions are distinct:
\[
\tau\ceq\tau^{\Un},\qquad
\epsilon\ceq\tau^{\{\emptyclass\}},\qquad
\varepsilon\ceq\tau^{\emptyclass}.
\]
The last descriptor is partial: no default value is supplied. The uncertain
value $U$ belongs to this alternative, not to the classical active kernel.

\section{Preserved Alternative 4: Farmer's PFOL}

\texttt{210\_\allowbreak partial\_\allowbreak functions\_\allowbreak semantics.tex} presents
Farmer's Partial First-Order Logic. Its iota descriptor satisfies
\[
(\iota x,\varphi)\downarrow\iff(\exists!x,\varphi),
\]
and partial values are compared with Kleene equality $\simeq$. These notions
must not be silently identified with the active total union evaluation.

\section{Preserved Alternative 5: Conglomerate-Valued Semantics}

\texttt{030\_\allowbreak alternative\_\allowbreak semantics.tex} retains the outdated subset semantics
with assignments of the form
\[
A:\mathrm{Var}\to\power(K).
\]
It is an independent historical semantics, not an interpretation of the
active formulas-as-types discipline.

\section{Reading and Maintenance Rules}

For the class-theoretic development, begin with the logical framework,
axioms, ordered pairs, and sethood, then continue to class operations,
ordinals, and recursion. For descriptors and partiality, read the uncertain
descriptor notes, the many-valued experiments, and the PFOL section as
separate approaches. For the connection with implementation, read the
classes-as-types discussion together with the language and proof-tree notes.

\paragraph{A dependency route through the constructions.}
For the recursive constructions, first read the elementary ordinal
comparison results in the ordinal chapter, then ordinal and membership
recursion in the induction chapter. Return to the ordinal chapter for
set-like relation recursion, collapse, and Hartogs' theorem; the recursive
pairing proof uses the membership-recursion result. The book retains its
historical chapter order, so some of these prerequisites occur after their
first application. The union argument has a different role: its ordinal
facts are supplied as local assumptions, and its proof does not invoke
later recursion results that themselves assume union. The finite and
hereditarily finite models in the class overview help distinguish these
assumptions from bare comprehension.

\paragraph{From concrete types to their universal properties.}
The classes-as-types chapter constructs Boolean, option, list, W-, and
M-type models in explicit set-sized settings. The language chapter
separately studies a specified simple syntax: type checking, substitution,
preservation, normalization, and confluence. Its Boolean operational
extension adds a two-point type and conditionals with both branches
typed; the new substitution, progress, normalization, and confluence
cases are proved separately, yielding decidable typed conversion.
The category chapter explains
recursion and corecursion through initial algebras and final coalgebras,
and derives fusion, induction, and coinduction principles. A semantic
model and a syntactic decision procedure are distinct results, even when
they use the same type names.

\paragraph{The analytic route.}
Metric compactness and finite-dimensional normed spaces supply the
initial topological preliminaries. The topology chapter also proves
proper ultrafilter extension, the ultrafilter criterion for compactness,
and set-indexed Tychonoff in an explicit classical setting with Choice. Measure theory constructs the integral and
proves its convergence theorems and function-space completeness.
Functional analysis then develops duality and Banach-space principles,
compact operators, uniform integrability, and the sequential
finite-measure Dunford--Pettis criterion. A further argument proves
the full topological equivalence for real $L^1$ on a finite positive
measure space: uniform integrability, compactness of the weak closure,
and the weak subsequence criterion are equivalent. Its prerequisites
include Hilbert-ball weak compactness via products and Riesz
representation, preservation of uniform integrability under weak
closure, and weak subsequences in weakly compact subsets of normed
spaces; uniformly controlled truncations provide the ultrafilter proof.
The probability applications compare weak, setwise, in-measure, and
total-variation convergence and prove that probability densities form
a weakly closed set, giving compactness results for their uniformly
integrable families.
The examples distinguish sequences from nets and finite measure from
infinite counting measure; those changes of setting matter to the
conclusions. Each chapter records the choice assumptions actually used.

The assembled book also includes exploratory sections from approaches 3--5;
these do not inherit the active implementation's conventions merely by
appearing after a mainline chapter. Examples include
\texttt{180\_\allowbreak many-valued.tex},
\texttt{210\_\allowbreak partial\_\allowbreak functions\_\allowbreak semantics.tex}, and
\texttt{030\_\allowbreak alternative\_\allowbreak semantics.tex}.
The chapter \texttt{020\_\allowbreak descriptors.tex} supplies the descriptor comparison and
its finite selection model.
The historical iota and family constructions introduce further local variants.
A heading marked as an outline or a proof sketch indicates the extent of the
current development. In particular, the planned Alexandrov and Strang
topics still require a specified theorem and hypotheses. The files
\texttt{025\_\allowbreak unified\_\allowbreak descriptors.tex} and
\texttt{035\_\allowbreak unified\_\allowbreak type\_\allowbreak semantics.tex} are retained only as rejected,
non-normative drafts marked \texttt{[OUTDATED]}.
\texttt{050\_\allowbreak unified\_\allowbreak interface.tex} provides navigation only: changing a
\verb|\topic| title does not and cannot switch foundations.
